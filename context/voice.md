## Which skill owns which format

| Format | Skill |
| --- | --- |

| Launch promo video | `launch-video` |
| Slack, ClickUp, Lark or email message to a colleague | `work-message-polish` |
| Voice pass on anything sent, posted or published | `humanizer` |

Not covered by a skill yet: tutorials and recipe walkthroughs.

## The voice in five lines

1. A developer explaining something to another developer. Plain words, specific details, the verb doing the work.
2. Say what the thing does and stop. No hype words, no drumroll before the point, no summary at the end.
3. Show the failure and the tradeoff. A post that admits what doesn't work yet is believed about what does.
4. Product appears where it earns its place. In a tech reference that is one section.
5. If a sentence would survive in a competitor's blog with the name swapped, it isn't saying anything. Cut it or make it specific.

## Per channel

- **agora.io blog.** Reference tone. Direct answer in the first 40 to 80 words. Named author and technical reviewer.
- **Launch copy.** Short. Lead with what a developer can now do. References for restraint: Vercel, Stripe, Linear.
- **X.** One idea per post. An outside developer's quote should sound like that developer and never like product copy.
- **Reddit.** A person describing what they built and what broke. Short, uneven, no brand.
- **Messages to colleagues.** Casual, peer to peer, question form for asks, no opener and no sign-off.
- **Video.** Dark, quiet, one typeface, real recordings carrying the proof.

## Hard bans everywhere

No em dashes or en dashes. No words from the humanizer poison list. No "not just X, it's Y". No groups of three for effect.
