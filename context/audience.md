# Audience

Stable context. June edits this file. Agents propose changes by pull request.

## Who we write for

1. **Developers building voice agents.** They can get a demo running. They get stuck when tuning it and when taking it to production.
2. **Developers who build with coding agents** (Claude Code, Cursor, Codex). They describe what they want and expect the agent to find the right SDK and a working example. For them, our docs, Skills and Recipes are read by the agent first and the person second.
3. **Developers adding classic real-time features** (calls, live streaming, classrooms) who now do it through a coding agent.
4. **AI search engines and assistants.** They decide whether Agora gets cited or recommended. They need a direct answer near the top, plain definitions, tables, an FAQ and text that renders without JavaScript.

Chinese-language developer content runs on its own track and is not covered here. `[JUNE: confirm]`

Use the wall as a source of topics and numbers. Don't name the conference in the headline.

## What they search for

Scenario keywords (the Top 10 series): customer support, hotel reception, travel booking, trial-class sales, medical triage booking, speaking coach, event confirmation, recruitment screening, customer survey, voice shopping.

Problem keywords (tech references): WebRTC vs WebSocket for voice agents, interruption handling and barge-in, turn detection, reducing latency, filler words, ASR keywords.

## What they don't respond to

- Bare integration walkthroughs with nothing to see at the end
- Polished promotional copy, on Reddit most of all
- Self-reported numbers with no public method
