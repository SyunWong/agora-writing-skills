# Case Studies: How Each Company Actually Writes

Per-company breakdown with what to borrow and what to skip. Sources are primary (their own posts/feeds) or first-hand analyses; verified July 2026.

## Vercel — the launch post machine

- **Signature**: "Introducing X: [benefit clause]" posts, 3-min reads, one headline metric (85% cost cut), benefit-titled sections, dense internal links to prior posts, zero-friction CTA ("no migrations required"), acknowledgments crediting prior art including competitors.
- **Borrow**: the 6-beat launch structure; the discipline of ONE headline number; internal link mesh (every launch references 3–4 previous launches — momentum becomes visible).
- **Watch out**: their tone is enabled by scale; a small company copying the polish without the density of shipped work reads hollow.

## Supabase — community-native distribution

- **Signature**: Launch Week (fixed timeline, flexible scope); the engineer who built it writes the post AND picks the channels; memes as audience selection; jargon as tribal signal; dark visuals; retweet-everything; no threads, no promo copy, low volume.
- **Borrow**: builder-writes-the-content rule; launch-things-twice; minute-level run sheets; "wrong answers only" style engagement posts; meme discipline (the joke targets your exact user's pain).
- **Watch out**: meme voice requires actually being of the community; forced memes from a corporate account do damage.

## Linear — craft as message

- **Signature**: changelog as flagship content; video-first entries; capability-first phrasing ("Move, label, or close multiple issues at once"); narrative continuity openers ("Today we're closing the loop"); dogfooding stats ("~30% of bug reports resolved"); thought leadership essays by named leaders with contrarian theses and uncomfortable specifics.
- **Borrow**: user-impact-first rewriting of every headline; continuity openers; internal usage stats as proof; the essay structure (thesis → reframe → own experience → extractable lessons).
- **Watch out**: their minimalism works because the product visuals are exceptional; with weaker visuals, add more explanatory copy.

## Resend — founder distribution + docs as marketing

- **Signature**: Zeno Rocha's personal account as primary channel (built since 2009, English-first since 2014); story-arc posts (doubt → belief → milestone → "back to work"); metric + insight posts; changelogs with code snippets and migration guides; 20k waitlist before launch; quality-over-category-noise narrative ("we don't need another email API" → focus on quality).
- **Borrow**: founder narrative formula; changelog-as-scrutinized-by-senior-engineer; the insight-attached-to-metric post format.
- **Watch out**: founder-led distribution takes years of consistent presence; start now, expect slow compounding.

## Cursor — product as the only channel

- **Signature**: near-zero marketing spend; product announcements outperform thought leadership on their accounts; named enterprise testimonials (Salesforce, PayPal) before feature explanations; 700+ community events, 300+ ambassadors.
- **Borrow**: lead with named social proof when you have it; company account = product news, personal accounts = opinions; invest in community programs over ad spend.
- **Watch out**: "zero marketing" only works with a product whose value is self-evident in the first session; most products still need the narrative layer.

## Stripe — prose quality as brand

- **Signature**: narrative memo culture (docs over slide decks, footnoted emails from the CEO); launch copy that defines the product in one sentence then shows what it enables; event-anchored launch batches (Sessions: 288 launches); ecosystem framing ("economic infrastructure", "agent-ready").
- **Borrow**: the one-sentence product definition discipline; treating writing quality as a hiring/culture issue, not a marketing task; category-level framing that positions the product inside a bigger economic story.
- **Watch out**: Stripe's authority lets them speak at civilization scale; smaller companies should frame one level up from their product, not five.

## Cross-company synthesis (the patterns that appear everywhere)

1. Artifact over adjective — demo, code, number, recording. All six companies.
2. Capability-first headlines. All six.
3. Real numbers, precisely stated, never rounded up for drama.
4. The builder's voice somewhere in the content chain.
5. Narrative continuity between releases.
6. Community amplification designed in, not hoped for.
7. Low-volume, high-density publishing. Nobody spams.
